How it works

Developed at Cornell.
Tested across 40+ countries.

Integrated Venture Engineering (IVE) was built by a coalition of academics, major corporations, and international investors — Cornell, Oxford, Cambridge, Barclays, BMW, Disney, P&G, DuPont, Mars, and the IFC — to answer one question: why do structurally broken ventures keep getting built? IVE applies the same design discipline that engineers aircraft and bridges to verify a venture's architecture before a penny is spent building it.

01

Define the Prime Commercial Opportunity

Not the product — the problem. Who is suffering, what are they actually losing, and why could a solution generate cash flows at scale? Getting this right is the foundation everything else is built on.

02

Verify three foundational requirements

Can you deliver at a cost below what customers will pay? Does your product remove the customer's single biggest measurable loss? Can you reach scale without running out of cash first? Three questions. All three must hold.

03

Simulate the financials before building

Apply costs to the operating model. Calculate the required price. Test it against what customers will actually pay. If the Financial Margin of Safety is below 60% — redesign now, not after three years.

Gate: FMOS ≥ 60%

What you'll know

The questions that matter
before you build anything.

Whether your unit economics can work

Is the cost of making, selling, and delivering your product structurally below what customers will pay — not in the optimistic scenario, but when reality is 20% harder than expected?

Whether customers will pay the required price

Not whether they find it interesting — whether the cost they're carrying right now is large enough that the required price is a rational fraction of their actual loss.

Whether you'll run out of cash before reaching scale

A venture can be profitable at scale and still die — if the gap between spending and revenue arrival is larger than it can survive. This surfaces it before it kills you.

Whether the architecture is structurally sound

A Financial Margin of Safety above 60% means the venture stays viable when real-world conditions are significantly worse than projected. Below 60% — the design needs work, not the execution.

About

An engineer who has learned to listen.

Tom Manuel is the founder of Tough Minds, Tender Hearts. Over the past decade, he has applied the IVE methodology to ventures across more than 40 countries — from social enterprises in sub-Saharan Africa to technology companies in the US and UK.

Before founding TMTH, Tom was VP at Barclays Bank and has worked with organisations ranging from early-stage startups to global institutions. His work has been featured in the Harvard Business Review and MIT Sloan Management Review.

IVE was developed at the Built2Hold Program, Center for Global Sustainable Enterprise at Cornell University — in collaboration with Oxford, Cambridge, and a coalition of corporations including Barclays, BMW, Disney, Pearson, P&G, DuPont, and Mars, and backed by the International Finance Corporation.

The methodology integrates that engineering rigour with the kind of deep listening most business frameworks ignore — because the answers a founder needs don't always come from analysis alone.
